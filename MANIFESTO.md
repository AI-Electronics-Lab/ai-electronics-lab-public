# AI Electronics Lab Manifesto

Status: public-facing draft  
Last updated: 2026-06-09

## 1. Useful electronics work needs honest evidence

AI Electronics Lab should help people explore electronics ideas with structured explanations, simulation-oriented artifacts, and visible caveats.

A simulation is not proof that hardware will work.

A generated schematic or report is not a substitute for engineering judgment.

The product is useful only when it makes evidence, assumptions, and limitations visible.

## 2. Clarity beats hype

The project should be understandable to learners, makers, junior and mid-level engineers, educators, and technical reviewers.

It should avoid language that sounds more certain than the system really is.

Prefer:

```text
simulation-oriented workflow
model evidence
generated artifact
requires review
prototype workflow
visible caveat
```

Avoid:

```text
guaranteed hardware correctness
AI-designed working circuit
production-ready design
engineer replacement
```

## 3. The goal is better engineering behavior, not magic

The project explores whether AI agents can help people practice better engineering habits:

- clarify the goal;
- define the model;
- inspect assumptions;
- run a small experiment;
- read the plot;
- compare before and after;
- document caveats;
- decide the next question.

The ideal result is not just an answer.

The ideal result is a reviewable workflow.

## 4. Senior judgment is valuable and hard to scale

Senior engineering judgment is scarce. Junior and mid-level specialists often need a way to move faster without hiding uncertainty.

AI Electronics Lab does not try to replace senior engineers.

It tries to make parts of senior-style reasoning more accessible and easier to review:

- explicit assumptions;
- visible artifacts;
- caveats;
- iteration history;
- structured comparison;
- clear boundaries between simulation and reality.

## 5. Human responsibility remains central

AI agents can help structure work, preserve context, generate artifacts, and propose next steps.

Humans remain responsible for:

- publication approval;
- engineering review;
- physical validation;
- safety decisions;
- product direction;
- claims made in public.

## 6. Public documentation is part of the product

README files, examples, limitations, FAQ entries, and release notes are not secondary.

They are product surfaces.

They should be reviewed, versioned, and kept aligned with real capability.

## 7. The project is also a human-machine R&D experiment

AI Electronics Lab is both a prototype product and a research surface for agent-assisted product development.

The project explores:

- AI agents in product work;
- Scrum-like iteration with human approval gates;
- runtime truth over superficial green checks;
- documentation as memory;
- public artifacts as trust surfaces;
- engineering work as a sequence of inspectable decisions.

## Short version

AI Electronics Lab is not about promising that AI can design hardware automatically.

It is about making electronics simulation workflows more structured, visible, caveated, and reviewable.
