# AI Electronics Lab Manifesto

Status: draft. Not approved for public publication yet.

## Principle 1: useful electronics work needs honest evidence

AI Electronics Lab should help people explore electronics ideas with structured explanations, simulation artifacts, and visible caveats. The product should not present a model or a simulation as proof that hardware will work.

## Principle 2: public claims must be bounded

Public-facing text must separate:

- current capability;
- known limitation;
- roadmap or future work;
- speculation or proposal.

## Principle 3: technical truth has a review path

Any claim about electronics correctness, SPICE behavior, schematic validity, calculation accuracy, units, safety, or hardware validity needs Technical review before publication.

## Principle 4: clarity beats hype

The public narrative should be understandable to hobbyists, students, makers, and engineers without promising more than the system can responsibly deliver.

## Principle 5: public documentation is part of the product

README files, limitations, examples, FAQ entries, and release notes should be treated as product surfaces. They should be reviewed, versioned, and kept aligned with real capability.
